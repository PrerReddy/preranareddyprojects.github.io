SECTION // 01 — COMPUTATIONAL PARADIGMS

1. Valence and Reinforcement Learning

Asymmetric Learning Rates ($\eta^+$ vs. $\eta^-$)

Human reinforcement learning is often valence-sensitive. Computational models parameterize this using asymmetric learning rates for positive vs. negative prediction errors. While adults generally exhibit a "positivity bias" ($\eta^+ > \eta^-$; Lin & Palminteri, 2020), emotional contexts shift these updating speeds.

RL-DDM Parameterization

In Reinforcement Learning Drift-Diffusion Models (RL-DDM), biases directly alter choice dynamics: higher $\eta^+$ accelerates approach learning, whereas higher $\eta^-$ amplifies avoidance (Pedersen et al., 2017).

PILOT DATA TAKEAWAYS
  • Decreased Learning Rate ($\eta$): Emotional feedback slows value updating regardless of direction, supporting context-modulated learning rate theories (Fontanesi et al., 2019; Pezzulo et al., 2015).
  • Decreased Decision Threshold ($a$): Faster, less cautious responding emerged in valenced vs. neutral blocks—suggesting emotional feedback may expedite decisions via heightened arousal.
  • Shift in Prior Bias ($z$): Negatively valenced symbols increased starting-point bias, indicating an avoidance/pessimistic expectancy (Fox et al., 2018).
SECTION // 02 & 03 — METHODS & PHARMACOLOGY

2 & 3. Hierarchical Bayesian Frameworks & Acetaminophen

2. Hierarchical Bayesian RL-DDM

Utilizing tools like JAGS, Stan, or HDDM (Wiecki et al., 2013) enables simultaneous estimation of individual and group-level parameters. Hierarchical MCMC allows robust comparison of models with vs. without valence-dependent parameters using credible intervals (Pedersen et al., 2017; Fontanesi et al., 2019).

3. Acetaminophen & Affective Processing

Beyond analgesia, acetaminophen (paracetamol) blunts emotional reactivity. Single-dose studies show diminished neural responses to social pain, lower late positive potentials (LPP) to emotional images, and reduced empathic distress (Randles et al., 2016)—indicating a broad inhibition of evaluative processing.

4. Acetaminophen and Learning Dynamics

Pearson et al. (2018) demonstrated that 1000 mg acetaminophen enhanced reflective (rule-based) learning while modestly impairing reflexive learning. EEG paradigms reveal reduced error-related positivity (Pe) amplitudes, indicating dampened conscious error evaluation (Randles et al., 2016).

SECTION // 05 — INTEGRATING PILOT FINDINGS

5. Working Hypotheses under Pharmacological Probe

If acetaminophen's evaluative blunting reduces valence sensitivity in our RL-DDM paradigm, we predict three main parameter shifts:

H1

Learning Rate Normalization

Normalization of learning rates ($\eta$) in valenced blocks back toward neutral control-block levels.

H2

Reduction in Prior Bias

A measurable decrease in valence-specific prior starting-point bias ($z$).

H3

Threshold Reversal

Attenuation or reversal of the decision threshold ($a$) decrease seen under emotional feedback—or global threshold increases driven by enhanced deliberative processing (Pearson et al., 2018).

SECTION // 06 — GAPS & CITATIONS

6. Novel Contributions & References

Summary of Project Novelty

To our knowledge, no prior study has combined RL-DDM analysis of probabilistic valence learning with an acetaminophen manipulation. This project uniquely unites a hierarchical RL-DDM approach to quantify parameter shifts with a pharmacological probe to test causal mechanisms underlying neurochemical bias modulation.
