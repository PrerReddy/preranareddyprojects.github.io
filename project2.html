Valence-Modulated Learning & Effects of Acetaminophen

A computational investigation into how affective context shifts reinforcement learning drift-diffusion parameters, evaluated alongside a pharmacological probe of evaluative blunting.

MODELING Hierarchical RL-DDM
PARADIGM Probabilistic Valence Task
COMPOUND Acetaminophen (1000 mg)
01 THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

Valence & Reinforcement Learning

Human reinforcement learning is inherently valence-sensitive. Standard computational paradigms represent this asymmetry by fitting separate learning rates ($\eta^+$ vs. $\eta^-$) for positive and negative prediction errors. While adult populations often exhibit a positivity bias ($\eta^+ > \eta^-$), emotional and developmental contexts frequently invert or dampen these dynamics (Lin & Palminteri, 2020).

$\eta^+$ / $\eta^-$

Learning Rate Asymmetry

Higher $\eta^+$ accelerates approach learning toward rewards; higher $\eta^-$ amplifies avoidance in punishment contexts (Pedersen et al., 2017).

$a$

Decision Threshold

Controls response caution. Lower thresholds speed up reaction times at the expense of choice accuracy.

$z$

Prior Bias

Shifts the starting point toward a choice boundary, reflecting expectancy driven by motivational valence (Fox et al., 2018).

KEY PILOT FINDINGS

Our pilot data revealed three distinct parameter shifts under emotional feedback: a general decrease in learning rate ($\eta$), a lowered decision threshold ($a$, indicating faster, less cautious choices), and an increased prior avoidance bias ($z$) for negative symbols.

02 METHODOLOGY

Hierarchical Bayesian RL-DDM Frameworks

By coupling reinforcement learning with Drift-Diffusion Modeling (RL-DDM) under a Hierarchical Bayesian framework (via JAGS, Stan, or HDDM), we simultaneously estimate individual and group-level parameters. Hierarchical Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling provides robust parameter recovery and allows formal comparison of models with vs. without valence-dependent parameters using 95% credible intervals (Wiecki et al., 2013; Fontanesi et al., 2019).

03 PHARMACOLOGY

Acetaminophen & Affective Modulation

Beyond its classical analgesic mechanisms, acetaminophen (paracetamol) induces broad evaluative blunting—reducing self-reported and neural responsiveness to both negative and positive emotional stimuli (Randles et al., 2016).

In cognitive tasks, acetaminophen has been shown to enhance reflective, rule-based learning while dampening error-related positivity (Pe) amplitudes in EEG (Pearson et al., 2018; Randles et al., 2016). Applying this pharmacological manipulation to an RL-DDM task provides a direct causal probe into how neurochemical blunting alters valence-driven decision parameters.

04 HYPOTHESES

Working Hypotheses

H1
Learning Rate Normalization:

Acetaminophen's affect-blunting will attenuate valence sensitivity, returning learning rates ($\eta$) in valenced blocks toward neutral control baseline levels.

H2
Prior Bias Reduction:

Valence-driven starting-point bias ($z$) toward negative or positive outcomes will decrease under acute acetaminophen.

H3
Threshold Reversal or Elevation:

The decrease in decision threshold ($a$) observed under emotional feedback will be attenuated—or elevated globally if acetaminophen selectively enhances deliberative caution.
